# Income, Food Affordability & Dietary Behavior Analysis

## Project Overview

Does healthy eating depend on income?

This project explores the relationship between household income, food affordability, and dietary behavior using real-world survey and pricing datasets.

The analysis investigates whether higher-income households are more likely to afford healthier foods, how food prices vary geographically, and whether healthier foods are consistently more expensive.

This project demonstrates practical skills in:

- Data cleaning
- Exploratory data analysis
- Feature engineering
- Risk and affordability analysis
- Data visualization
- Business insight generation

---

## Problem Statement

Access to healthy food is a major public health and socioeconomic issue.

Key questions explored:

- How does household income affect food affordability?
- Are healthier foods more expensive than unhealthy alternatives?
- Do higher-income households consume healthier diets?
- Are there geographic differences in food affordability?

---

## Key Insights

### 1. Income significantly affects affordability
Households with higher disposable income showed better food affordability.

### 2. Healthy food often costs more
Fresh fruits, vegetables, and seafood showed higher average costs than some unhealthy categories.

### 3. Geographic inequality exists
Some metro regions had significantly higher monthly food basket costs.

### 4. Lower affordability may impact dietary choices
Affordability constraints likely influence consumption behavior.
